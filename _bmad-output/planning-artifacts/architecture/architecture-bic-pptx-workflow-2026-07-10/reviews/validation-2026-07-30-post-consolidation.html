Is the consolidated spine BMad-conformant?

Target: architecture-bic-pptx-workflow-2026-07-10/ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md — the single project spine, after the Epic 16 child spine was folded in and Epic 20 landed as AD-16..AD-19. 2026-07-30.

Structurally sound and BMad-shaped in the large — but not conformant in four ways, one of them the kind a consistency contract cannot have.

The consolidation itself is right, and it made the project more BMad-default than before. What is off-pattern is what the spine now carries: a self-contradiction left in place on purpose, a template-mandated section missing, rationale where the template says decisions only, and a memlog that no longer holds the decisions its own spine states.

What passes — stated so it is not re-litigated

  • lint_spine.py: 0 findings. AD ids ascending, none duplicated or reused, every block carries Binds / Prevents / Rule, every Stack row pinned, no placeholders, no dangling cross-references.
  • One spine per project is the BMad default, and the live folder now matches the configured run_folder_pattern exactly. The earlier deviation — an epic folder hand-created while the pattern was never overridden — is resolved by the merge rather than papered over.

  • Brownfield ratification is strong. Every mechanism the Epic 20 decisions name was read from source this session, not recalled.

Critical

C1 The spine contradicts itself, and the contradiction is parked rather than resolved

In ## Consistency Conventions, the State row strikes through “services are immutable once presented” and flags it; ## Open items item 1 says the replacement wording is not written. AD-16 states the opposite in force.

A spine’s single job is to be the one consistency contract. A contract holding a struck-through clause plus a note about what it ought to say is not a contract — two readers can honestly reach opposite conclusions, which is the failure mode the artifact exists to prevent. This was defensible while two spines existed: the child carried a correction, the parent held the stale text, and the conflict was visibly cross-document. Folding them into one file removed that alibi.

BMad also has no state for this. The skill’s triage is explicit — blockers get resolved one at a time, everything else is deferred with a revisit condition in the memlog. There is no third bucket where a known-false statement stays in the spine.

Fix: write the corrected State row. The owner supplied the substance on 2026-07-30 — freeze at creation, supporting data entry rather than the generated deck, Sync permitted on any service. This is a transcription, not a decision.

High

H1 Capability → Architecture Map is missing, and it is template-mandated here

The template marks that section “Present when a spec drove this run” and calls it “the consistency auditor’s checklist.” Two specs drove this run and both sit in sources: — spec-slide-artifact-model and spec-artifact-registry-authoring, the latter carrying CAP-1..CAP-8 as the entire reason AD-16..AD-19 exist. The section is absent.

Not a formatting nit: that map is the mechanism that catches an uncovered capability, and it would have caught H2 without needing a reviewer at all.

Fix: add it — one row per CAP-1..CAP-8 plus the Epic 16 areas, each naming where it lives and which AD governs it.

H2 CAP-1 has no invariant: nothing fixes that deck order stops living in code

CAP-1’s success clause is “…without editing TypeScript plan constants.” The spine never says that. AD-7 makes buildSlidePlan the only order source; AD-16 makes the snapshot the sequence input. Together they imply the registry supplies order — but neither forbids a liturgical rule continuing to live as a literal in the planner, which is exactly what { skipTitle: true } does at three sites in src/lib/slide-plan.ts today, and exactly what Story 20.1 (absorbing 19.1) must remove.

Two units can comply fully and diverge: one moves suppression into registry data, the other adds a fourth skipTitle literal for a new liturgical case. Both obey every AD.

Fix: an AD fixing that per-slide sequence and liturgical flags are registry data, with the planner applying rather than storing them — which is also what makes Story 20.1’s AC checkable.

H3 Rationale sits in the spine where the template says the memlog

The template guide is unambiguous: “Decisions, not rationale (rationale lives in the memlog). Carry shape in diagrams; prose only where it must.” Default output is a terse build substrate. Four blocks violate it — and they are the four newest.

BlockOff-pattern content
AD-16“What the snapshot is for — and what it is not”: ~200 words of purpose plus three consequence bullets
AD-17“Operational consequence this rule must carry”
AD-18“Pre-first-deploy waiver” narrative
AD-19Four extra bullets, including catalog-key spelling advice and two SPEC reconciliations addressed to a different skill’s pass

Some of it is load-bearing and belongs somewhere: AD-17’s private-override trap is a real constraint, and AD-16’s “not obliged to keep old snapshots renderable” genuinely binds a migration. But the reasoning, the declined alternatives, and notes addressed to a future bmad-spec run are memlog and hand-off material. As written these ADs read as meeting minutes, and terseness is what keeps a small agent from drifting.

Fix: compress each to Binds / Prevents / Rule plus at most one binding clause; reasoning to the memlog (much is already there), cross-skill notes to sprint-status.yaml.

Medium

M1 The spine is no longer distilled from its own memlog

BMad’s Update flow resumes from .memlog.md, “the authority on what was decided”, and Finalize step 1 distills the spine from it. AD-11..AD-19 were instead transcribed from the rendered Epic 16 spine. This memlog holds the fold-in decisions but not the nine decisions the spine states, whose record lives only in ../architecture-epic-16/.memlog.md — which the live spine’s companions: does not reference (it lists CASE-STUDY.md alone).

Consequence: a future resume reads this memlog, finds no record of AD-11..AD-19, and either re-derives or drops them.

Fix: list ../architecture-epic-16/.memlog.md as a companion of record and say in the spine that AD-11..AD-19 were decided there. Cheaper than back-filling entries, and it does not fake a history.

M2 Story-level detail in an initiative-altitude spine

altitude: initiative keeps features coherent, and the skill says even an epic spine “does not expand per-story detail.” AD-16..AD-19 name Stories 20.1, 20.2, 20.5, 20.7 and 20.8, and one bullet is addressed to “the bmad-spec pass” — two altitudes below this document.

This was the foreseen cost of the merge, left unmitigated. Not fatal on its own; combined with H3 it is why the spine has grown from a contract into a briefing.

Fix: keep story pointers only where they identify who owes an unresolved piece; drop the rest.

M3 The tombstone invents frontmatter and occupies a scanned filename

../architecture-epic-16/ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md declares type: architecture-spine-tombstone, status: superseded and superseded_by:. None exist in BMad — the template’s type is architecture-spine and status is draft · final. Worse, it keeps the name ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md inside spine_output_path, and the skill’s activation step 4 scans that path for a prior run to resume. A future run may offer to resume a tombstone.

Fix: rename outside the scanned name (e.g. SUPERSEDED.md) with status: final and the supersession stated in the body, or move the folder under superseded/.

M4 Open items is a section BMad does not have

Related to C1 but distinct. The template’s vocabulary for undecided things is Deferred, each item carrying the reason it can wait; the skill’s triage routes the remainder to the memlog with a revisit condition. A bespoke Open items section creates a third bucket no downstream skill or reviewer knows to read.

Fix: resolve item 1 (C1); move item 2 to Deferred with its revisit condition, or resolve it too — it is a one-line clarification to AD-9’s own Rule.
